# Safety and scope

## What this is

An **educational and research-prototyping** kit. It is **not a medical device**, not a diagnostic
tool, and not intended for clinical monitoring or clinical decision-making.

PPG signals and every score derived from them depend strongly on sensor placement, motion,
contact pressure, ambient light, skin tone, individual physiology, and calibration. Treat all
outputs as teaching artifacts.

## General rules

- Use only for education and research prototyping.
- Do not use any output for medical diagnosis or clinical decisions.
- Keep sensor contact gentle. Excess pressure hurts and degrades the signal.
- Do not collect or publish participant data without appropriate consent and approvals
  (see [`DATA_POLICY.md`](DATA_POLICY.md)).

## Project 04 (voluntary apnea) — additional rules

- It is a **short, voluntary, supervised** classroom-style activity only.
- The participant starts and ends the breath-hold and stops at the first sign of discomfort.
- **Never** encourage longer holds, competition, or repeated holds.
- Stop immediately on dizziness, pain, discomfort, anxiety, tingling, or shortness of breath.
- Not for anyone pregnant, or with a cardiovascular, respiratory, or seizure condition, or who
  is uncomfortable with the activity for any reason. Offer them the simulator scenario instead.
- The displayed "SpO2" is an **uncalibrated educational proxy**, not a measurement. Do not use it
  to reassure or alarm anyone.

## If you adapt this for a course

Follow your institution's ethics/IRB process for any human-subject data you keep or analyse
beyond a live in-class demo.
